## Introduction

Croatia has emerged as one of the premier destination countries in the European Union for Nepali skilled workers, students, and professionals seeking long-term opportunities. However, after receiving your Croatian Work Permit (Dozvola za boravak i rad) or university acceptance letter, your paperwork journey is far from over. 

Before the Embassy of Croatia in New Delhi or Croatian Ministry of Interior (MUP) will issue your Type D long-stay visa or Temporary Residence Permit (TRC), every critical document originating in Nepal—including your Police Clearance Certificate (PCC), academic transcripts, birth certificate, and marriage records—must be fully legalized. Because Nepal is not a signatory to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention, standard Apostille stamps cannot be issued in Kathmandu. Instead, your documents must move through a rigorous, multi-tiered superlegalization chain spanning Kathmandu and New Delhi.

## Key Requirements / What Changed

The complete document superlegalization pipeline for Croatia is:

**Issuing Office (Police HQ / University / Ward) → Line Ministry (MoEST / MoHA if applicable) → Nepal MOFA Attestation → Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi → Embassy of Republic of Croatia, New Delhi**

- **3-Month Validity Rule for PCC:** The Croatian Ministry of Interior (MUP) strictly requires Police Clearance Certificates to be fresh—typically issued within **3 months** of your final embassy or TRC submission date. An expired PCC leads to immediate visa rejection.
- **Mandatory MoEST Endorsement for Degrees:** Academic transcripts, provisional certificates, and diplomas issued by Tribhuvan University (TU), Kathmandu University (KU), or NEB/HSEB cannot go directly to MoFA. They must first receive verification from the **Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MoEST)** in Sanothimi or Keshar Mahal.
- **The Embassy Chain in New Delhi:** Because Croatia does not maintain a full consular legalization desk in Kathmandu, your MoFA-stested documents **must** be counter-verified by the Embassy of Nepal in New Delhi before the Croatian Embassy in New Delhi will accept them for superlegalization.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Obtain Base Documents** — Apply for a fresh electronic Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) via the Nepal Police Online Portal. For academic records, collect original transcripts and character certificates from your university or examination board.
2. **Line Ministry Verification (MoEST / Civil Dept)** — Submit educational credentials to the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MoEST) for official verification. Civil status documents (Marriage/Birth) must carry proper Ward and executive Municipal seals.
3. **Nepal MOFA Online Portal & Attestation** — Register your details on the Consular Services Department (NEATS) online portal, upload high-resolution scans, book a physical appointment, and obtain the official QR-coded authentication sticker at Tripureshwor, Kathmandu.
4. **Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi Counter-Verification** — Express-courier the MoFA-stamped documents to New Delhi, where the Nepalese Diplomatic Mission verifies and affixes its mandatory endorsement seal.
5. **Embassy of Croatia, New Delhi Superlegalization** — Submit the verified documents alongside official fee payments and appointment receipts to the Embassy of the Republic of Croatia in New Delhi for final consular superlegalization.
6. **VFS Submission & TRC Processing** — Hand over your fully superlegalized documents to VFS Global or the relevant immigration authority for your Type D Visa and Croatian TRC card processing.

**Estimated timeline:** roughly 20–30 working days end-to-end, depending on MoEST queue times, courier transit between Kathmandu and Delhi, and embassy appointment availability.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Skipping MoEST Verification:** Submitting academic degrees to Nepal MoFA without the prior Ministry of Education verification stamp, resulting in instant counter rejection.
- **Timing Mismatches on PCC:** Applying for the Police Clearance Certificate too early, causing it to cross the 90-day age limit while waiting for Delhi embassy appointments.
- **Bypassing the Nepal Embassy in Delhi:** Submitting MoFA-attested documents directly to the Croatian Embassy in New Delhi without first obtaining the Nepalese Embassy in Delhi's cross-verification seal.
- **Spelling & Name Discrepancies:** Minor variations between the Romanization of names across citizenship certificates, educational transcripts, and passports.
