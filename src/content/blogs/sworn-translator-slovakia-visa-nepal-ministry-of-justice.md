## Introduction

Getting a Slovakia study or work visa from Nepal is not only about your acceptance letter or job contract. The Slovak Embassy will also look closely at your supporting documents, such as your Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) and academic certificates. If these are legalized or translated the wrong way, your file can be sent back.

The most common mistake is simple: **translating documents too early, or using a translator who is not officially recognised in Slovakia.** This guide explains what a sworn translator is, how the Slovak Ministry of Justice register works, and the exact order your documents must follow.

## What Is a Sworn Translator in Slovakia?

In Slovakia, a sworn translator (*súdny prekladateľ*) is a person authorised under **Act No. 382/2004 on experts, interpreters and translators** and entered in the official list kept by the **Ministry of Justice of the Slovak Republic**.

* Translators and interpreters have separate sections in the register.
* Registered translators use an **official ID card and an official stamp** issued through the Ministry.
* The register is **public and free to search** by name, language, registration number or location. It is only available in Slovak.

Slovak embassy guidance for Nepal-based applicants states that all documents must be translated by an official translator from the Ministry of Justice list. A translation by a general agency or a friend who knows Slovak will not meet this requirement.

## Key Requirements: What the Slovak Embassy Expects

* **Where to apply:** Citizens of Nepal submit national visa (D visa) applications at the **Embassy of the Slovak Republic in New Delhi**.
* **Legalization first:** For Nepal-issued documents, the embassy states that documents must be legalised by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and superlegalised by the Slovak Embassy in New Delhi **before any other action, including copying and translation**.
* **Official translation:** Foreign-language documents must be translated into Slovak by a Ministry of Justice registered translator.
* **Complete file:** The embassy does not accept incomplete document sets, and missing or incorrectly prepared documents can lead to refusal.
* **Validity:** Documents must still be valid on submission. Embassy notices have required documents to be no more than 90 days old, so always confirm the current rule.
* **Timing:** National visa applications for residence purposes should be submitted at least 30 days before travel, and the embassy decides within 30 days of submission.

## Why Nepal Documents Need Superlegalisation

Nepal is not part of the Hague Apostille Convention. A simple apostille is therefore not available, and your documents go through a chain of verification that ends with the Slovak Embassy in New Delhi. This is why the order of steps matters so much.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Get your original documents ready.** For example: PCC, SEE/+2/Bachelor's certificates and transcripts, birth or marriage certificates, and experience certificates for workers.
2. **Issuing-authority verification and MoFA attestation (Nepal).** Documents are verified at the relevant office and then attested by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Kathmandu.
3. **Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi.** Documents receive the embassy stamp.
4. **Slovak Embassy, New Delhi.** The embassy completes superlegalisation.
5. **Sworn translation into Slovak.** Only now do the documents go to a translator registered with the Ministry of Justice. Ask for the translator's stamp, signature and registration details on the translation.
6. **Final audit and submission.** Check names, dates, spelling and validity before your embassy appointment.

## How to Verify Your Translator

1. Open the Ministry of Justice register of translators (*Zoznam znalcov, tlmočníkov a prekladateľov*).
2. Search by name, language or registration number.
3. Confirm that the translator is entered for your language pair and still active.
4. Check that the final translation carries the official stamp and signature.

## Common Mistakes That Delay Applications

* Translating before legalization is complete.
* Using a translator who is not on the Ministry of Justice register.
* Submitting documents that are too old by the time of your appointment.
* Name or date mismatches between the passport and the translation.
* Submitting an incomplete file.
