Whether you are applying for a foreign student visa, registering a marriage abroad, or submitting corporate contracts to overseas banks, foreign authorities will rarely accept raw Nepali-language paperwork. They demand a **certified translation** that carries official legal standing.

Under **Section 19(b) of the Notary Public Act 2063 (2006)** and **Rule 18 of the Notary Public Rules 2063**, document translation is a core statutory function of a licensed Notary Public in Nepal.

This guide details the legal frameworks governing certified translation in Nepal, how to avoid embassy rejections, statutory fee caps, and how to get your documents translated without visiting an office.

---

## What Is a Certified Document Translation in Nepal?

A certified translation in Nepal is more than converting Devanagari script into English or another foreign tongue. It is a legally recognized document verified under oath and recorded in the official notary register (**Section 23**).

### A valid certified translation includes:
1. **Source Document Copy:** A clear photocopy of the original document.
2. **Translated Text:** A line-by-line translation, including stamps, watermarks, and seals.
3. **Translator Affidavit / Notarial Attestation:** The official seal, registration number, and signature of a licensed Notary Public or sworn translator.
4. **Register Entry:** Serial registration under the Notary Public Council of Nepal.

## The 2-Tier Translation Model in Nepal

Nepal's legal framework divides official document translation into two distinct procedural tiers based on the target language:

+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  NEPAL NOTARIAL TRANSLATION SYSTEM                      |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
|               TIER 1               |               TIER 2               |
|      Nepali ↔ English Pairs        |        Other Foreign Languages      |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| • Direct Notary Attestation        | • Translator Affidavit + Notary    |
| • Governed by Rule 7 & Rule 18     | • Governed by Sec. 19 & Chain      |
| • Single output, fastest turnaround| • Required for Japanese, Korean,   |
|                                    |   German, Arabic, French, etc.     |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+

### Tier 1: Nepali ↔ English (Direct Notary Translation)
For English-to-Nepali or Nepali-to-English translations, a licensed Notary Public who has passed the examination under **Rule 7 of the Notary Public Rules 2063** can directly translate and attest the document. 

The translated text, source document reference, and notarial stamp sit on a single official sheet. This is the standard route for most visa, study, and migration applications.

### Tier 2: All Other Foreign Languages (Translator-Affidavit Chain)
For languages such as Japanese, Korean, Mandarin Chinese, German, French, Spanish, Italian, and Arabic, the process uses a two-step chain of trust:
1. A qualified, sworn translator prepares the translation and signs a **Sworn Affidavit of Accuracy**.
2. A Notary Public attests the translator's identity and signature under **Section 19**.

> **Note:** Both tiers carry equal legal validity and are accepted by all embassies, courts, universities, and visa application centers (such as VFS Global and TLScontact) in Kathmandu.

## Statutory Translation Fee Rules in Nepal

Translation fees in Nepal are regulated by **Rule 20 of the Notary Public Rules 2063**, which establishes statutory fee caps based on document classification:

* **Civil Records (Per Paper Cap):** Citizenship, birth/marriage certificates, ward records, and land ownership extracts (*Lalpurja*).
* **Legal & Corporate Documents (Per Page Cap):** Court judgments, corporate Articles of Association (AOA/MOA), contracts, and financial audits.
* **General Documents (Per Paper Cap):** Recommendation letters, employment certificates, and No Objection Certificates (NOC).

For non-English translation pairs (Tier 2), the overall cost consists of the **professional translator fee** (calculated by word count and complexity) added to the **statutory notarial attestation fee**.

## 6 Common Reasons Translations Get Rejected at Embassies

1. **Incorrect Translation Tier:** Attempting to get a non-English language (like Japanese or Korean) directly stamped by a notary without an attached Translator Affidavit.
2. **Inconsistent Name Transliteration:** Spelling "Suresh" on the translation when the applicant's passport spells it "Suresha". **We cross-check all names against your passport.**
3. **Omitted Stamps or Seals:** Leaving official government seals, watermarks, or signatures untranslated in Devanagari. Every seal must be rendered as `[Stamp: Name of Issuing Authority]`.
4. **Poor Source Document Quality:** Translating from blurry, low-resolution phone screenshots instead of clear scans.
5. **Unformatted Layout:** Failing to mirror the exact structural layout of original birth certificates or academic transcripts.
6. **Missing Consular Attestation Chain:** Submitting a notarized translation abroad without completing the subsequent **Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA)** attestation chain.

## Certified Translation vs. Full Legalization Chain

Understanding how far your document needs to be processed depends on where it will be used:
[Notarized Translation] ➔ [Line Ministry Stamp] ➔ [MOFA Attestation] ➔ [Target Embassy Stamping]

* **Notarized Translation:** Required for most domestic uses and local embassy submissions in Kathmandu.
* **MOFA Consular Attestation:** Required if the translated document is leaving Nepal for official use in a foreign government office or abroad.
* **Embassy Legalization:** The final authentication step carried out by the destination country's embassy in Kathmandu or New Delhi.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What makes a translation "certified" in Nepal?
A certified translation is a translated document that has been legally attested by a licensed Notary Public under Rule 18 of the Notary Public Rules 2063 (for English) or accompanied by a sworn Translator's Affidavit attested under Section 19 (for other languages).

### Can a Nepal Notary directly translate Japanese or Korean?
No. Under Rule 7, direct notary translation is limited to Nepali and English. All other languages require a sworn translator to execute an affidavit of accuracy, which the notary then attests.

### How long does a certified translation take?
Routine Nepali-to-English translations take **30 to 60 minutes** for walk-ins or same-day processing online. Foreign language pairs (such as German, Japanese, or Arabic) take **1 to 2 business days**.

### Are translated documents valid indefinitely?
Yes. A notarized translation does not expire unless the underlying source document itself carries an expiration date (such as a Police Clearance Certificate or temporary NOC).
