## Introduction

Buying or selling a used motorcycle or scooter in Nepal is an exciting transaction, but completing the legal ownership transfer—known in Nepal as **Naamsari (ownership transfer)**—is mandatory. A simple verbal agreement, cash transaction, or handing over the Bluebook does not constitute a legal sale under Nepalese law. Until the vehicle is officially re-registered in the buyer's name at the Transport Management Office (*Yatayat Vyawastha Karyalaya*), the original registered owner remains legally responsible for traffic violations, accidents, and unpaid annual road taxes.

The core legal mechanism of Naamsari is **Sanaakhat (legal identity and transfer verification)**—a mandatory in-person session where both the buyer and seller declare the sale, verify their identities, and sign the official transport ledger in front of a Transport Officer. 

Here is the ultimate guide covering document requirements, tax rates, third-party insurance rules, government transfer fees, and step-by-step Naamsari and Sanaakhat procedures.

---

## Required Documents: Buyer (Party A) vs. Seller (Party B)

To ensure a smooth approval at the Yatayat office counters, both parties must bring their specific documentation:

### 👤 Party A: Buyer (*Kharidkarta*)
- **Original Citizenship Certificate (*Nagarikta*)** + 2 clean photocopies.
- **Passport-Size Photographs:** Minimum 2 recent auto-size photos.
- **Signed Ownership Transfer Application Form (*Naamsari Form*):** Available at the Yatayat counter or downloaded via the provincial transport portal.

### 👤 Party B: Seller (*Vikrikarta*)
- **Original Vehicle Registration Booklet (*Bluebook*):** Must be up-to-date with no missing pages.
- **Original Citizenship Certificate (*Nagarikta*)** + 2 clean photocopies.
- *(Note: Party B only needs to present their original Nagarikta for identity verification during Sanaakhat. Passport-size photos are generally not required for the seller during standard transfers, though carrying one is recommended).*

> **Special Cases:** If the bike is owned by a company, an official **Board Resolution Letter** and **Certificate of Incorporation** are required. If the motorcycle was financed through a bank, a formal **Bank Release Letter (*Haq Chhod Letter*)** must be obtained before Naamsari can proceed.

## Prerequisites: Mandatory Tax & Third-Party Insurance Clearance

Under Nepalese transport laws, **no vehicle ownership transfer can be executed if there are pending annual taxes, expired insurance, or unpaid traffic fines**.

### 1. Annual Vehicle Road Tax Clearance
Vehicle tax in Nepal is calculated based on engine displacement (CC). Taxes must be cleared for the current fiscal year before initiating Naamsari:

* **Up to 125cc:** NPR 3,000 / year
* **126cc – 150cc:** NPR 5,000 / year
* **151cc – 225cc:** NPR 6,500 / year
* **226cc – 400cc:** NPR 12,000 / year
* **401cc – 650cc:** NPR 25,000 / year
* **Above 651cc:** NPR 35,000 / year
* **Electric Two-Wheelers (EVs):** NPR 2,000 to NPR 3,000 depending on motor wattage.
* **Annual Bluebook Renewal Charge:** NPR 300.

**Late Tax Payment Penalties:**
If tax renewal has expired, late fines apply:
- First 30 days past due: **5% fine** on tax
- Up to 45 days: **10% fine** on tax
- Within same fiscal year: **20% fine** on tax
- Beyond fiscal year (up to 5 years): **32% annual fine** on tax.

### 2. Mandatory Third-Party Insurance Clearance
A valid **Third-Party Motor Insurance Policy** is compulsory for Naamsari. Standard annual third-party insurance premiums in Nepal:
- **Up to 149cc:** NPR 1,710
- **150cc – 250cc:** NPR 1,935
- **Above 251cc:** NPR 2,160

*(Note: Once Naamsari is completed, the buyer must transfer or issue a new insurance policy under their own name within 7 days).*

## Step-by-Step Naamsari & Sanaakhat Process
[Clear Outstanding Tax & Insurance] ➔ [Document Verification] ➔ [Sanaakhat Signature Desk] ➔ [Fee Payment] ➔ [Blue Book Endorsement & Collection]

### Step 1: Visit the Respective Yatayat Office
Both buyer and seller must physically present themselves at the **Yatayat Vyawastha Karyalaya (Two-Wheeler Division)** where the bike is registered (e.g., Gurjudhara, Ekantakuna, Radhe Radhe, or provincial zonal offices).

### Step 2: Tax & Penalty Settlement Counter
Present the Bluebook to the revenue desk. If annual road taxes, renewal fees, or late penalties are due, clear them first at the cash counter.

### Step 3: Chassis & Engine Physical Verification
Bring the motorcycle to the physical inspection station at the Yatayat premises. An official inspector cross-checks the **Chassis Number** and **Engine Number** stamped on the bike frame against the details printed in the Bluebook.

### Step 4: The *Sanaakhat* (Legal Transfer Desk)
Both Party A (Buyer) and Party B (Seller) stand before the designated Transport Officer:
- The officer inspects the original *Nagarikta* (Citizenship) cards of both parties.
- Buyer and seller sign and apply right and left thumbprints (*Laha Chhap*) on the official government transfer ledger and application form.
- The officer authenticates the transaction (*Sanaakhat done*).

### Step 5: Payment of Ownership Transfer Fees
Proceed to the payment voucher desk:
- **Official Naamsari / Ownership Transfer Fee:** Ranging between **NPR 500 to NPR 1,500** depending on provincial transport rules and vehicle engine capacity.
- Collect the official computerized payment receipts.

### Step 6: Updated Bluebook Collection
Submit the signed application, payment vouchers, and original Bluebook to the dispatch counter. The Yatayat office prints the buyer's details on the new ownership page, applies official stamps, and returns the updated **Bluebook** registered under Party A's name.

## Common Pitfalls & Risks

- **Missing the Actual Registered Owner:** Skimming the *Sanaakhat* process by purchasing through middleman brokers without the true registered owner present is illegal. Without the registered owner's original citizenship and live signatures, ownership cannot be transferred.
- **Accumulated Back-Taxes:** Tax penalties compound every year up to a 32% penalty rate. Always inspect the last renewal stamp in the Bluebook prior to making payments.
- **Financed Vehicles:** Always ensure the bank has issued an official release stamp and *Haq Chhod* letter before buying a financed bike.
