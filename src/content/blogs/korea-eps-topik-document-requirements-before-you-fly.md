Introduction

Getting to South Korea under the Employment Permit System (EPS) is one of the most sought-after paths for Nepali workers — better wages, strong legal protections, and a structured government-to-government process instead of relying on individual recruitment agents. But EPS is also a long process with several distinct document checkpoints, run jointly by Nepal's Department of Foreign Employment (DoFE) and Korea's HRD Korea (Human Resources Development Service of Korea).

Passing the EPS-TOPIK language exam is often treated as the finish line, but it's really just the first checkpoint. From there, workers go through a health check, get listed on an employer roster, sign a labour contract, apply for the actual visa, and complete mandatory pre-departure training — each stage with its own paperwork. Missing or delaying any one document at any stage can knock a candidate out of the current hiring cycle entirely, since employer selection windows move fast once you're on the roster.

This guide walks through exactly what's needed at each stage, so you know what to prepare and when.

Key Requirements: The Documents You'll Need at Each Stage

At EPS-TOPIK registration:

Nepali citizenship certificate (original and photocopy, both sides)
Valid Nepali passport (machine-readable, valid well beyond the exam date — renew early if it's close to expiring)
Recent passport-size photo, plain white background
Educational certificate (SEE/SLC or equivalent, minimum requirement)
Exam fee payment voucher from the designated bank
Basic health declaration form (a self-declaration — the full medical exam comes later)

After passing EPS-TOPIK and the sector skill test, for your Job Application Form:

Passport
Medical fitness report from a DoFE-authorized medical center
Police clearance certificate (no criminal record)
Application fee receipt

Once an employer selects you and your contract is finalized:

Signed standard labour contract (issued through DoFE)
Certificate of pre-entry/EPS education
Medical fitness certificate (final)
Shram Swikriti (Nepal's foreign employment labour permit/approval)
Citizenship certificate
Valid passport, for the E-9 visa application at the Korean Embassy in Maharajgunj, Kathmandu

Before departure:

Pre-departure orientation completion certificate
Insurance enrollment and welfare fund deposit proof
Confirmed air ticket
Step-by-Step Process

Step 1: Register for EPS-TOPIK Applications open through DoFE's EPS Korea Branch in Gwarko, Lalitpur, and are submitted online. You'll need your citizenship certificate, passport, a recent photo, and your SEE/SLC certificate ready before you start the form — incomplete documentation is one of the most common reasons applications get rejected at this stage. Pay attention to photo specifications (plain white background, both ears visible, correct file size) since the online portal is strict about these details.

Step 2: Sit the EPS-TOPIK exam The test is 50 multiple-choice questions on Korean reading and listening, out of 200 total marks. The official pass mark is 80/200, though in years with heavy competition for limited placements, a meaningfully higher score improves your chances of being matched with an employer quickly. Bring your original citizenship certificate, passport, and admission card to the exam center — these are checked before you're allowed to sit.

Step 3: Pass the sector-specific Skill and Competency Test Passing EPS-TOPIK alone doesn't put you on the employer roster. You also need to pass a practical skill assessment relevant to your target sector (manufacturing, agriculture, construction, etc.) before you're allowed to submit your Job Application Form.

Step 4: Complete your medical examination This happens at a DoFE-authorized hospital — commonly Patan Hospital, the Civil Service Hospital, Korea-Nepal Friendship Hospital, or Sukraraj Tropical and Infectious Disease Hospital in Nepal. TB clearance is mandatory, along with general fitness screening. This medical report becomes one of the documents in your Job Application Form.

Step 5: Submit your Job Application Form and enter the worker roster With your passport, medical report, police clearance, and application fee receipt, you're entered into HRD Korea's roster (also called the EPS Pool). From here, Korean employers search the roster by sector, score, and experience — you cannot apply to a specific employer or choose who selects you.

Step 6: Wait for employer selection This stage varies widely — anywhere from a couple of weeks to several months, depending on your sector and the current hiring quota. There's no document action required here, but it's worth using this time to make sure your passport won't expire during the process and that any documents with a validity window (like your medical certificate) won't lapse before you're matched.

Step 7: Sign your standard labour contract Once an employer selects you, DoFE facilitates signing of the standard EPS labour contract, which sets out your wage, working hours, and contract term. Read this carefully — it's the document that becomes the basis of your E-9 visa application and your legal protections in Korea.

Step 8: Apply for your Shram Swikriti (labour approval) Before any Nepali citizen can legally depart for foreign employment, Nepal's Department of Foreign Employment issues a labour permit — commonly referred to as Shram Swikriti. This is a mandatory domestic approval, separate from the Korean visa itself.

Step 9: Apply for the E-9 visa at the Korean Embassy, Kathmandu With your finalized contract, medical certificate, citizenship certificate, Shram Swikriti, and passport, you apply for the E-9 Non-Professional Employment Visa at the Korean Embassy in Maharajgunj. The visa is generally issued through a Confirmation of Visa Issuance process coordinated between your employer and Korean immigration, rather than a standard walk-in consular application.

Step 10: Attend mandatory pre-departure orientation DoFE's EPS Korea Branch in Gwarko runs a multi-day (typically around three days) training covering Korean workplace culture, your rights as a foreign worker, survival-level Korean, and emergency contacts. This isn't optional — it's a required document checkpoint, and you'll need proof of completion before you're cleared to fly.

Step 11: Complete insurance enrollment and welfare fund deposit Required insurance coverage and a welfare fund contribution are typically arranged through DoFE before departure — keep the receipts, as you may need to show proof at the airport or upon arrival in Korea.

Step 12: Final document check before flying Before heading to the airport, confirm you're carrying: valid passport with your E-9 visa, signed labour contract, medical certificate, Shram Swikriti, pre-departure training certificate, insurance proof, and your confirmed air ticket. Keep both originals and photocopies — some are needed at Korean immigration on arrival, others by your employer.

Common Mistakes That Cost Candidates Their Slot
Letting the passport run close to expiry during the months-long wait between EPS-TOPIK and employer selection
Missing the medical exam's validity window — if too much time passes between your medical certificate and your visa application, it may need to be redone
Treating pre-departure orientation as optional or scheduling travel before completing it
Name inconsistencies between citizenship certificate, passport, and contract — even small spelling differences can cause delays at the visa stage
Submitting blurry or incorrectly sized photo/passport scans at the online registration stage, which can get an application rejected outright
