## Introduction

Starting a company in Nepal has become faster and more digital — but it's still not a one-click process. Registration now runs primarily through the **Office of the Company Registrar's (OCR) CAMIS portal**, governed by the **Companies Act, 2063 (2006)**. Yet the process still touches multiple government offices, requires a mandatory digital signature, and carries strict document and compliance requirements that trip up first-time founders — especially those with foreign shareholders.

## Key Requirements

- **Governing body:** Office of the Company Registrar (OCR), under the Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies
- **Portal:** CAMIS (camis.ocr.gov.np)
- **Minimum for a Private Limited Company:** 1 shareholder, NPR 1 lakh paid-up capital
- **Government fee:** From NPR 1,000 (varies with capital)
- **Mandatory Digital Signature (DSC):** Class 3 DSC required for all directors under the Electronic Transactions Act
- **Typical timeline:** 7–21 working days from name reservation to certificate issuance

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Name Reservation** — Check and reserve your company name on the OCR/CAMIS portal.
2. **Draft the MOA & AOA** — Memorandum and Articles of Association tailored to your business structure.
3. **Prepare & Upload Documents** — Citizenship certificates, PAN, photographs, shareholder/director details, registered office proof, and no-objection letters. Directors must also obtain their Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate.
4. **OCR Review & Verification** — The registrar examines your filing and may raise queries; timely responses keep the process moving.
5. **Pay Fees & Receive Certificate** — Once approved, pay the government fee and receive your QR-verified Certificate of Incorporation.
6. **PAN/VAT Registration** — Register with the Inland Revenue Department to legally begin operating.
7. **Ward Office Registration** — Register your business address with the local municipal ward office.
8. **3-Month Post-Registration Compliance** — Hold your first board meeting, appoint an auditor (if required), issue share certificates, file the return of allotment, set up statutory registers, register with the Social Security Fund, and open a corporate bank account.

**For foreign investors:** an added approval layer applies — Department of Industry / Investment Board Nepal approval, a Foreign Investment Approval Certificate, and Nepal Rastra Bank clearance for capital transfer — before standard OCR registration can proceed.
