## Introduction

If you're a Non-Resident Nepali (NRN) in the US, UK, or Europe, you've probably asked this question in a Facebook group at least once: *"Do I have to fly back to Nepal just to sign paperwork for my land?"*

It's one of the most common blockers NRNs face — a parcel of ancestral land needs to be sold, a family inheritance needs to be settled, or a dispute needs a signature, and the person who needs to sign is thousands of miles away. Flying home isn't cheap, and it isn't always possible around work and family commitments.

The good news: Nepali law already has a solution for this, and it doesn't require a plane ticket.

## What Changed / What You Need to Know

The document that solves this is commonly called a **Manjurinama** — a notarized Power of Attorney or consent letter that your local Nepal Embassy or Consulate can issue. It authorizes a trusted person in Nepal (usually a family member or lawyer) to sign, register, and act on your behalf for:

- Selling or transferring land
- Family property settlements
- Inheritance matters

**One important eligibility note:** this route is generally available to Nepali citizens who hold a valid Nepali passport. Some embassies suspended Power of Attorney attestation services for **naturalized foreign nationals of Nepali origin** starting in 2023 — if you've taken foreign citizenship, check current rules with your specific embassy before assuming this route applies to you.

## Step-by-Step Process

**At the Embassy (Steps 1-3):**
1. **Draft the Manjurinama** — A Nepal-based lawyer prepares the document to match Malpot (Land Revenue) Office requirements, including exact plot numbers, boundaries, and Lalpurja details.
2. **Book your embassy appointment** — At the Nepal Embassy or Consulate in your country of residence.
3. **Sign in person before the Ambassador** — This step cannot be skipped or done remotely; only the Head of Mission can certify the document.

**Back in Nepal (Steps 4-7):**
4. **Embassy attestation** — The signed document is legalized for use in Nepal.
5. **Couriered to your representative** — Sent to the trusted person you've named in Nepal.
6. **Registered at the Malpot Office** — The document must be formally registered before your representative can act on it.
7. **Representative completes the transaction** — Sale, settlement, or transfer proceeds at the Malpot Office on your behalf.
