## Going to Moldova for Work? Your PCC Must Be Legalized — Here's How

Moldova is an increasingly popular destination for Nepali skilled workers, particularly in construction, manufacturing, agriculture, and hospitality. But before you can secure your **Type D (Long Stay) Work Visa**, Moldovan immigration authorities require a critical document: a legalized **Police Clearance Certificate (PCC)**.

If you're a Nepali citizen, this is where most applicants get stuck. Moldova has specific legalization requirements for Nepal-issued PCCs that are different from many other European destinations — and getting this wrong means visa rejection.

This guide gives you the exact, verified 3-step chain.

---

## Why Standard Attestation Isn't Enough

Moldova is a member of the Hague Apostille Convention (since March 16, 2007). In theory, an apostille certificate should suffice for documents used in Moldova. However, **Nepal is not a member of the Hague Apostille Convention**. This means no authority in Nepal — not MoFA, not any notary — can issue a valid apostille stamp.

Because Nepal documents cannot be apostilled, the Moldovan government requires them to go through a **full consular legalization chain**, ending at the Embassy of the Republic of Moldova in New Delhi. This has been explicitly confirmed on the VFS Global visa information page for the Moldova Embassy:

> *"PCC legalization from Embassy of the Republic of Moldova would be required for Nepalese, Bangladeshi, and Sri Lankan applicants for Type D visa categories."*

---

## The Verified 3-Step PCC Legalization Chain for Moldova

### Step 1: Obtain Your PCC from Nepal Police

Apply for your Police Clearance Certificate through the **OPCR (Online Police Clearance Report) portal** at `opcr.nepalpolice.gov.np`.

- The online application is **free of charge**
- Processing takes **2–3 working days**
- The certificate can be downloaded digitally
- Ensure the PCC is in **English** (the Moldova Embassy requires documents in English)

**Important:** The PCC must be recent. Check the validity requirements with the Moldova Embassy — most embassies require a PCC issued within **3–6 months** of the visa application date.

---

### Step 2: MoFA Attestation — Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Nepal

Take your original PCC to the **Department of Consular Services, Ministry of Foreign Affairs**, located at Tripureshwor, Kathmandu.

- MoFA will affix an official **attestation sticker/stamp** verifying the authenticity of the Nepal Police seal and signature
- Processing time: **1–3 working days**
- This step is **mandatory** — the Moldova Embassy will not accept a document without prior MoFA attestation

---

### Step 3: Legalization at the Embassy of the Republic of Moldova, New Delhi

This is the final and most critical step. The **Embassy of the Republic of Moldova in New Delhi** is the only Moldovan diplomatic mission authorized to legalize Nepal-issued documents for use in Moldova.

**Documents Required at the Embassy:**
1. Completed application form for document legalization (downloadable from `india.mfa.gov.md`)
2. Original PCC (in English), with MoFA attestation already applied
3. Applicant's original passport
4. ID of authorized representative (if submitting through an agent)
5. Notarized Power of Attorney (if submitting through a representative)
6. Proof of payment of legalization fee

**Legalization Fee:**
- Individuals: **€25 (approx. NPR 3,700 / INR 2,800)**
- Legal Entities: **€45 (approx. INR 5,040)**
- Payment is via **bank transfer to the Embassy's account** — cash is not accepted

**Appointment Booking:**
- Email: `consul.newdelhi@mfa.gov.md`
- Include a scanned copy of your document in the email for pre-verification
- The service is provided **on the day of the appointment**

**Embassy Address:**  
Embassy of the Republic of Moldova, New Delhi, India (Chanakyapuri diplomatic enclave area)

## Optional: Nepal Embassy Attestation in New Delhi

The Moldova Embassy's official page states documents must be legalized by MoFA **"and/or"** the diplomatic mission of Nepal in India. In some cases, agents and applicants include a **Nepal Embassy (New Delhi) attestation** step between MoFA and the Moldova Embassy as an additional layer of verification.

**We recommend confirming this with the Moldova Embassy directly** at `consul.newdelhi@mfa.gov.md` before your submission, as requirements can be updated. Apostille Sewa's pre-submission audit covers this check.

## Common Mistakes That Lead to Rejection

- **Submitting an expired PCC** — always check the 3–6 month validity window before starting the chain
- **Missing MoFA attestation** — the Moldova Embassy will reject documents not previously attested by Nepal MoFA
- **Sending documents in Nepali** — all documents must be in **English** or accompanied by a certified English translation
- **Making a direct payment at the Embassy** — fees must be transferred by **bank transfer in advance**; walk-in cash payments are not accepted
- **Not booking an appointment** — the Moldova Embassy's consular section works by appointment only
