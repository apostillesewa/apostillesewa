## Introduction

You have your acceptance letter or job offer, and now the embassy checklist says "official translation required." Some agents say you need a **certified translation**. Others say you need a **sworn translation**. Many first-time applicants end up paying twice because they bought the wrong one.

The two terms are not interchangeable. This guide explains the difference, what you can get in Nepal, what you can only get abroad, and how to know which one your visa needs.

## Certified Translation in Nepal: What It Is

In Nepal, a certified translation is a translation attested by a licensed **Notary Public** under the Notary Public Act 2063. Notaries are regulated by the **Nepal Notary Public Council**, and translators must pass the Council's examination to receive a translation license.

A certified translation normally includes:

* The translation, matched page by page to your original document
* The notary's seal and signature
* A certificate stating that the translation is accurate

Notary firms in Kathmandu report that, under the Council's examination policy, notaries attest **Nepali to English and English to Nepali** translations directly. Other languages usually need a separate route. If you search for "notary translation Kathmandu," this is the service you will find.

**Best for:** documents that must go from Nepali into English, such as citizenship certificates, birth certificates, marriage certificates and some academic records, when the receiving authority accepts English.

## Sworn Translation: What It Is

A sworn translation is a translation made by a translator who holds an **official appointment from the destination country**. The title and the rules differ by country:

* **Spain:** only a *traductor jurado* registered with the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC) can produce a sworn translation.
* **France:** the translator must be an approved judicial expert listed by a Court of Appeal or the national list of the Cour de cassation.
* **Slovakia:** the translator must be on the Ministry of Justice register.

This is why the phrase "sworn translator Nepal" can mislead you. Some Nepali advertisements use "sworn" loosely to describe a notary-attested translation. A true sworn translator is appointed in the **destination country**, not in Nepal.

## The Germany Exception

Germany shows why you must read the embassy checklist. The German Embassy in Kathmandu states that there are **no sworn translators in Nepal** and that the embassy cannot translate documents or notarize third-party translations. Its checklists accept documents in **German or English**, or with a certified translation. For German authorities inside Germany, translators sworn in Germany are generally expected, and they are listed in German state justice databases.

## Quick Comparison

| | Certified translation (Nepal notary) | Sworn translation (destination country) |
|---|---|---|
| Who issues it | Licensed Nepal Notary Public | Translator appointed by that country |
| Typical language pair | Nepali and English | Into the destination language |
| Examples | Many English-accepting embassies | Spain, France, Slovakia |
| Where to verify | Nepal Notary Public Council | Official register of that country |

## Which One Does Your Visa Need?

1. **Your document is already in English and the embassy accepts English.** You may need no translation, only legalization.
2. **Your document is in Nepali and the embassy accepts English.** A Nepal certified translation is usually the right choice.
3. **The embassy requires the national language** (for example Spanish, French or Slovak). You need a sworn or officially registered translator from that country.

Always check the embassy checklist first. Authorities decide their own rules, and missing or incorrectly prepared documents can lead to refusal.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Read the embassy checklist** and note the required language and translator type.
2. **Check legalization first.** For Spain, documents must be legalized or apostilled before sworn translation, because the translator also translates the stamp. Slovakia's embassy likewise asks for legalization before translation.
3. **Choose the correct translation type** using the guide above.
4. **Verify the translator.** A Nepal notary should hold a Council license. A sworn translator should appear on the destination country's official register or list.
5. **Check the final file.** Names, dates and spellings must match your passport exactly. Confirm the stamp, signature and registration details are on every required page.

## Common Mistakes

* Buying a Nepal notary translation when the embassy needs a sworn translator
* Translating before legalization is complete
* Using a translator who is not on the official register
* Name or date mismatches between the passport and the translation
* Trusting an agent who promises "100% acceptance" without reading your embassy's checklist
