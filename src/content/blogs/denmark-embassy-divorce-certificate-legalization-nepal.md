## Introduction

If you need your Nepali divorce certificate or court decree recognized in Denmark — for remarriage, family reunification, civil registration, or immigration — the document can't just be handed over as-is. Because Nepal is not part of Denmark's Hague Apostille framework, your divorce certificate must pass through a 5-step chain covering two government offices in Kathmandu and two embassies in New Delhi before Danish authorities (including SIRI) will accept it.

## Key Requirements / What Changed

The full legalization chain is:

**Ward Office / District Court → Law Book Management Board (Translation) → Nepal MOFA Attestation → Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi → Embassy of Denmark, New Delhi**

- Court judgments and Nepali-language divorce decrees **must** be translated into English by the official **Law Book Management Board (Kanoon Kitab Byabastha Samiti)** — private/notary translations are rejected.
- The **Embassy of Denmark in New Delhi** will not accept your document unless it already carries the **Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi** verification seal.
- Names, dates, case numbers, and passport details must match exactly across every document — mismatches are one of the most common rejection reasons.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Obtain the Divorce Certificate or Court Decree** — from your Ward Office (local registration) or District Court (Faisala/Milapatra). Confirm seals and signatures are clear.
2. **Official English Translation** — via the Law Book Management Board. Government-certified only.
3. **Nepal MOFA Attestation** — apply online through the Department of Consular Services, book an appointment, and receive the QR-coded authentication sticker.
4. **Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi** — cross-verifies the MOFA stamp; mandatory before the Danish Embassy will touch the document.
5. **Embassy of Denmark, New Delhi** — final legalization appointment and fee; once complete, the certificate is valid for Danish civil registries, courts, and immigration (SIRI).

**Estimated timeline:** roughly 12–24 working days end-to-end, depending on MOFA and embassy appointment availability.

## Common Pitfalls

- Using a private/notary translation instead of the Law Book Management Board
- Trying to submit directly to the Danish Embassy without the Nepal Embassy (New Delhi) seal
- Name spelling mismatches between the court decree and passport
