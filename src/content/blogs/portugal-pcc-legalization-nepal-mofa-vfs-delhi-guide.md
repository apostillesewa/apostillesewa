## Introduction

If you're applying for a Portugal D1 work visa, or you're already living in Lisbon or Porto and need to renew your status through AIMA, one document tends to cause the most delays: your Police Clearance Certificate (PCC). Unlike a straightforward local errand, a Nepali PCC destined for Portugal has to cross two countries and three government offices before it's accepted — and most applicants don't find that out until they're already stuck.

## The Jurisdiction Problem

The Consular Section of the Embassy of Portugal in New Delhi holds jurisdiction over documents originating from Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Maldives, and Sri Lanka. There is no shortcut through Kathmandu alone — every document in this region must be routed through New Delhi for the final consular stamp.

## The 3-Step Legalization Chain

**Step 1: Nepal MoFA Attestation (Kathmandu)**
Your original document — most commonly the Police Clearance Certificate — must first be authenticated and stamped by Nepal's Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

**Step 2: Embassy of Nepal Counter-Legalization (New Delhi)**
Once MoFA has stamped it, the document travels to India, where it's legalized by the Embassy of Nepal in New Delhi — a step that's easy to overlook but mandatory.

**Step 3: VFS Delhi Portugal Application Center**
Only after both prior steps are complete can the document be physically submitted to the VFS Delhi Portugal Application Center, where Portuguese Consular Services apply the final legalization.

## Key Rules You Can't Miss

* **Originals only** — photocopies or unverified prints are rejected outright.
* **Representatives are allowed**, but they must carry their own ID, a copy of the applicant's ID, and a signed Power of Attorney letter.
* **PCC validity window is 90 days** from the date of issuance — an expired PCC is automatically rejected.
* **Minors under 16** do not need to produce a criminal record at all.
* **Submission hours are limited** to 9:00 AM–12:00 PM at VFS Global / the Embassy of Portugal in New Delhi.

## Who This Really Affects

**Applicants already in Portugal (AIMA/regularization):** You need a fresh PCC to extend your status, but you can't process it from Lisbon or Porto — it has to physically move through Kathmandu and then Delhi.

**D1 visa applicants still in Nepal:** Coordinating a three-country pipeline while preparing to relocate is exhausting, and missing a stamp — or letting your PCC pass its 90-day window — leads directly to VFS delays or refusal.
