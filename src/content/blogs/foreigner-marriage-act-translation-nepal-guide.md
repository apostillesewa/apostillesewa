When a foreign citizen registers a **Court Marriage in Nepal** at a District Court (such as Kathmandu, Lalitpur, or Bhaktapur District Court), Nepali family law requires proof that the foreign applicant possesses full legal capacity to marry under their home country's laws.

Because District Courts operate exclusively in Devanagari (Nepali), the bench cannot take judicial notice of foreign laws in English, French, Japanese, or Arabic. Under Nepali evidence practice, foreign statutory law is treated as a **verifiable fact** that must be proved on record through certified translations.

This guide details the legal translation requirements for foreign Marriage Acts, capacity certificates, required statutory excerpts, and how to prevent court delays.

---

## Why District Courts Require Foreign Marriage Act Translations

To register a marriage under the **National Civil Code 2074 (Muluki Devani Samhita)**, the judge must verify three legal conditions regarding the foreign applicant:
1. **Minimum Marriageable Age:** Compliance with age consent rules (typically 20 years under Nepali law).
2. **Monogamy & Free Status:** Proof that no prior subsisting marriage exists.
3. **Prohibited Degrees of Relationship:** Proof that the marriage does not violate bloodline or affinity restrictions.

## The 2-Tier Translation Framework (Rule 18 & Rule 7)

Translations submitted to District Courts must be executed under **Section 19(b) of the Notary Public Act 2063** and **Rule 18 of the Notary Public Rules 2063**.

### Tier 1: English-Language Statutes (Direct Notary Translation)
If the home country's Marriage Act is published in English (e.g., USA, UK, Canada, Australia, India, Singapore, New Zealand, Kenya, South Africa), a licensed Notary Public who has cleared the Council's translator examination under **Rule 7** translates the text directly from **English to Nepali**.

### Tier 2: Non-English Languages (Translator-Affidavit Chain)
If the home country's Civil Code or Family Law is in Korean, Japanese, Mandarin Chinese, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian, or Arabic, the process follows a two-step legal chain:
1. A qualified, sworn translator prepares the Nepali translation and signs an **Affidavit of Accuracy**.
2. A Notary Public attests the translator's identity and signature under **Section 19**.

## Documents Required for the Court File

To complete your marriage file for the District Court bench, prepare certified translations of the following items:

1. **Foreign Marriage Act Excerpt:** Consolidated text in force on the translation date.
2. **Capacity Certificate:** Certificate of No Impediment (CNI), Single Status Certificate, or Affidavit of Free Status issued by your embassy or home-country civil registry.
3. **Identity Records:** Passport biographical page, valid Nepal entry visa stamp, and photo ID.
4. **Dissolution Records (If Previously Married):** Certified divorce decrees or spouse death certificates with consular authentication.

> **Crucial Requirement:** Supporting Single Status Certificates issued abroad must be **apostilled or consular-authenticated** by the foreign country's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Nepali Embassy before being translated into Nepali.

## 5 Mistakes That Cause Court Application Rejections

1. **Translating Outdated Laws:** Submitting outdated statutory text without incorporating recent legislative amendments.
2. **Name Transliteration Mismatches:** Spelling the foreign applicant's name inconsistently in Devanagari compared to the passport spelling.
3. **Translating Unauthenticated Certificates:** Translating a Single Status Certificate before it has undergone proper consular authentication.
4. **Omitting the Translator Affidavit:** Submitting a Tier-2 translation (e.g., French or Japanese) without an attached, notarized Translator's Affidavit of Accuracy.
5. **Over-Translating Irrelevant Sections:** Translating procedural or administrative chapters that judges do not ask for, increasing costs and file review times.

## Fee Structure for Marriage Act Translations

Fees for certified translations are governed by statutory caps under **Rule 20 of the Notary Public Rules 2063**:

* **Legal Documents (Statutes & Court Documents):** Statutory per-page fee cap applied to statutory translation.
* **Civil Records (Certificates & Passports):** Statutory per-paper fee cap applied to birth certificates, Single Status letters, and passports.
* **Translator Labor (Tier 2 Languages):** Professional translation labor fees for non-English language pairs calculated per word count prior to execution.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Why can't the District Court accept my Marriage Act in English?
Nepali courts operate officially in Devanagari. Under Nepali evidence rules, foreign statutory laws must be submitted as certified Nepali translations to be entered into the court record as evidence.

### Does Apostille Sewa perform the court marriage ceremony?
No. Court marriages are solemnized directly by the District Court Judge. Apostille Sewa provides the certified Nepali translations and supporting affidavits required to complete your court application file.

### How long does a Marriage Act translation take?
English-to-Nepali translations take **1 business day**. Non-English statutes (such as German, Korean, or Japanese) take **1 to 2 business days**.

### Do I need to translate my foreign Single Status Certificate?
Yes. Both your home country's Marriage Act excerpts and your Single Status Certificate (CNI) must be translated into certified Nepali and attached to the court petition.
