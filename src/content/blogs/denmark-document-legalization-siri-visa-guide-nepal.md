## Introduction

Whether you are applying for a Danish Residence and Work Permit under the Pay Limit or Positive List Schemes via the Danish Agency for International Recruitment and Integration (SIRI), enrolling in a Danish university, or reuniting with your family under Danish immigration laws, your documentation must meet strict compliance standards. 

Because Nepal is not a member of the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention, Danish authorities, civil registries (Folkeregister), and SIRI will reject any Nepali document carrying a standard Apostille or plain notary seal. Instead, all personal, civil, and educational records originating in Nepal must go through a comprehensive multi-step consular legalization chain across Kathmandu and New Delhi before they are legally accepted in Denmark.

## Key Requirements / What Changed

The mandatory legalization chain for Denmark-bound applicants is:

**Local Issuing Authority (Ward / University / Police HQ) → Line Ministry (MoEST / Kanoon Kitab if applicable) → Nepal MOFA Attestation → Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi → Royal Danish Embassy, New Delhi**

- **Mandatory 5-Step Verification:** Denmark strictly requires a double-embassy transit. Your document must be authenticated by the Embassy of Nepal in New Delhi before the Royal Danish Embassy in New Delhi will apply its final legalization stamp.
- **Strict Translation Regulations:** Any document issued purely in Nepali (such as court decrees, divorce certificates, or local ward civil records) must be translated into English exclusively through the official **Law Book Management Board (Kanoon Kitab Byabastha Samiti)** or certified government desks—private notary translations are systematically rejected.
- **Strict Validity Window for Police Clearance Certificates (PCC):** SIRI and the Danish Embassy require Police Clearance Certificates to be fresh—issued within **3 months** of your final visa or residence permit application submission.
- **Educational Pre-Verification at MoEST:** Transcripts and diplomas issued by Tribhuvan University (TU), Kathmandu University (KU), National Examinations Board (NEB), or CTEVT cannot proceed directly to MoFA without prior verification from the **Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MoEST)**.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Obtain Official Base Documents** — Collect your original certificates, degree transcripts, or civil status documents (Birth Certificate, Marriage Registration, Divorce Decree) from the respective Ward Office, District Court, or university. Apply online for a fresh Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) via the Nepal Police portal.
2. **Line Ministry & Official Translation** — Educational documents must be submitted to MoEST for verification stamps. Nepali-language court judgments or civil certificates must be officially translated into English by the Law Book Management Board.
3. **Nepal MOFA Online Portal & Attestation** — Submit an application through the Department of Consular Services (NEATS) portal in Kathmandu, select an appointment, and obtain the physical QR-coded authentication sticker at the Tripureshwor counter.
4. **Embassy of Nepal, New Delhi Verification** — Securely transport the MoFA-attested originals to New Delhi, where the Nepalese High Commission cross-verifies the MoFA sticker and applies its mandatory seal.
5. **Royal Danish Embassy Legalization (New Delhi)** — Submit the Embassy-verified documents to the Royal Danish Embassy in New Delhi (or its designated consular processing desk) alongside official embassy legalization fees for final superlegalization.
6. **SIRI / University / VFS Submission** — Attach your fully superlegalized documents to your online SIRI application (nyidanmark.dk), Danish university application, or VFS Global visa submission package.

**Estimated timeline:** roughly 18–28 working days end-to-end, depending on MoEST queue times, courier transit between Kathmandu and New Delhi, and embassy appointment schedules.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Attempting Direct Submission to the Danish Embassy:** Submitting MoFA-stamped documents to the Royal Danish Embassy in New Delhi without first obtaining the mandatory verification seal from the Embassy of Nepal in New Delhi.
- **Using Private Notary Translations:** Relying on uncertified local translation shops instead of the Law Book Management Board for court or civil documents.
- **Allowing Documents to Expire in Transit:** Poor scheduling that leads to your PCC passing its 90-day validity mark while waiting for New Delhi consular slots.
- **Data Discrepancies:** Name, date of birth, or citizenship number mismatches across your passport, Nagarikta, academic transcripts, and civil certificates.
