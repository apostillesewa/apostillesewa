If you're a foreigner hoping to live in Nepal for more than a few months at a time, a tourist visa won't get you there. Tourist visas are for visits — they don't let you own a company, draw income, or build a life here on a rolling basis. The legal path for long-term residency tied to business activity is the business visa, issued to foreign investors who put approved capital into a Nepal-registered company.

This is one of the most common questions we get from clients: *"I want to live in Nepal — what does it actually take, and how much do I need to invest?"* The honest answer is: **it depends entirely on your sector.** This guide breaks down exactly how the process works, what the investment rules are, and how the visa lets you stay year after year.

---

## Key Requirements: What the Investment Threshold Actually Is

This is the part most people get wrong, so let's be precise about it.

For most industries, Nepal's Foreign Investment and Technology Transfer Act (FITTA) 2075 sets a **minimum foreign direct investment (FDI) threshold of NPR 20 million** (roughly USD 150,000) per foreign investor. This applies to manufacturing, hospitality, general trading, and most conventional business sectors.

For **Information Technology and software-related businesses**, the rules are different. Since a Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies notice issued in October 2023, **IT companies have no minimum investment threshold.** This covers software development, IT services, data processing, business process outsourcing (BPO), digital platforms, and similar tech-driven businesses.

This is why some investors are able to set up a Nepal company, bring in a comparatively modest amount of capital — in the range of a few thousand dollars up to around USD 10,000–20,000 — and still qualify for an approved foreign investment and, from there, a business visa. If your business falls into a general commercial category rather than IT/software, don't assume the lower figure applies to you — confirm your sector classification before you commit to a structure.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Choose your business structure and confirm your sector's investment threshold**  
   Decide what kind of company you're registering and confirm — in writing, from the Department of Industry or a qualified advisor — whether your sector requires the NPR 20 million general minimum or qualifies for the IT-sector exemption. Getting this wrong at the start can derail the entire application.

2. **Apply for Foreign Investment Approval**  
   Before you register the company, you (or your local advisor) submit an application to the Department of Industry (through the FITT Unit / OSSC for smaller investments, or the Investment Board of Nepal for large-scale projects above NPR 6 billion). This includes a project report, proof of the source of funds, and your passport and background documents.

3. **Register the company**  
   Once investment approval is granted, you register the company at the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR), along with the Memorandum and Articles of Association, and complete local ward-level and tax (PAN) registration.

4. **Open a company bank account in Nepal**  
   The registered company needs a Nepali bank account before any investment funds can be brought in.

5. **Wire the investment through formal banking channels**  
   This step matters more than people expect. The invested capital must enter Nepal through the official banking system — not cash, not informal transfers — so that it can be properly recorded.

6. **Report the inflow to Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB)**  
   Nepal's central bank has to record the capital as officially approved foreign investment. This step is what makes the funds "real" in the eyes of immigration and tax authorities — skip it, and your investment won't count toward your visa application, even if the money has technically arrived.

7. **Get your recommendation letter**  
   With the investment verified, the Department of Industry issues a recommendation letter confirming you're an approved foreign investor.

8. **Apply for the business visa at the Department of Immigration**  
   Submit the recommendation letter along with your company documents and passport at the Department of Immigration in Kathmandu.

9. **Receive your visa**  
   Business visas are typically issued for a set period and are renewable annually, as long as your company remains active and the investment stays in place. Larger, long-established investments can sometimes qualify for multi-year validity.

## Staying in Nepal Long-Term: What Renewal Actually Looks Like

A business visa isn't a one-time grant of long-term status — it's a renewable arrangement tied to your company staying active. Each year (or at whatever interval your visa is issued for), you'll need to demonstrate that:

* The company is still operating and in good standing
* The approved investment remains in the business
* Your tax and regulatory filings are current

Once your investment has been formally brought in and reported to Nepal Rastra Bank, it becomes part of your company's working capital — meaning it can be used for legitimate business expenses like rent, salaries, equipment, and operations, the same as any other company funds. The key is that it has to go through the proper approval and reporting steps first. Investors who skip the NRB reporting step, or who try to bring funds in informally, often run into trouble at renewal time when authorities can't verify the paper trail.
