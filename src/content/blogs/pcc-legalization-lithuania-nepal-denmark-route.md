## Introduction

Moving to Europe is a massive milestone — but navigating international bureaucracy can quickly turn that excitement into stress. If you're a student or worker from Nepal preparing to head to Lithuania, you've likely run into a major hurdle: legalizing your Police Clearance Certificate (PCC).

Because Nepal is not a member of the Hague Apostille Convention, a standard apostille stamp won't work for Lithuania. Instead, your PCC must go through a precise, multi-country process known as **Double Legalization via the Denmark Route**. Without this exact authentication, your Temporary Residence Permit (TRP) or university enrollment can be rejected by MIGRIS, the Lithuanian Migration Department.

Here's everything you need to know to navigate the process smoothly.

## Why the Denmark Route Is Required for Lithuania

Most countries rely on the Apostille Convention to verify foreign documents quickly. Nepal hasn't signed that treaty, so Lithuanian authorities require an alternative validation chain.

Lithuania has no physical embassy in Kathmandu. Instead, the **Embassy of Nepal in Copenhagen, Denmark** is the channel accredited for Lithuania-related document legalization. That single fact is what creates the three-step chain below — and it's also the detail most applicants don't discover until their documents are already stuck somewhere in the process.

## The 3-Step Legalization Process

To make your PCC fully compliant with MIGRIS, it needs to pass through three separate government bodies, in order:

1. **MoFA Attestation (Kathmandu)** — Before your PCC can leave Nepal, it needs verification and digital stamping at the Department of Consular Services, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in Kathmandu.
2. **Legalization via the Nepal Embassy in Denmark** — This is where most applicants get stuck. Your MoFA-attested PCC has to be physically transported to Copenhagen, with fees paid in Danish Krone (DKK) and close coordination with embassy staff.
3. **Lithuanian MoFA Counter-Legalization (Vilnius)** — Once the Nepali Embassy in Denmark has authenticated the document, it travels to Vilnius, where the Lithuanian Ministry of Foreign Affairs performs the final counter-legalization — making it valid for submission to MIGRIS.

*Embassy fees, processing times, and accreditation details can change, so it's worth reconfirming current requirements directly with the relevant embassy before you start.*

## The Hidden Challenges of Doing It Alone

Handling this independently from Nepal is risky and slow. The most common roadblocks:

- **International courier risk** — shipping original, irreplaceable identity documents across Europe
- **Foreign currency logistics** — paying embassy fees in DKK or EUR from a Nepali bank account
- **Tight timelines** — a single delayed step can push back your visa processing enough to miss a university intake or job start date
