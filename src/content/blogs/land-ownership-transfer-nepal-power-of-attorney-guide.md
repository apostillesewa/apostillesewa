## Introduction

Transferring land ownership (*Jagga Naamsari*) in Nepal traditionally requires the physical presence of both the buyer and seller at the local **Land Revenue Office (*Malpot Karyalaya*)** to sign the transfer deed (*Rajnama*) before a Land Revenue Officer. 

However, many property owners—especially Non-Resident Nepalis (NRNs), foreign workers, and students living abroad—cannot physically return to Nepal just to complete a property transaction.

**Can land ownership transfer be done without the principal landowner's physical presence in Nepal?** 

**Yes, absolutely.** Under the Muluki Civil Code, 2074 of Nepal, a landowner can legally authorize a trusted relative, representative, or legal proxy in Nepal via a formal **Special Power of Attorney (*Adhikrit Warisnama / अधिकृत वारिसनामा*)**. Once executed and attested, your authorized attorney (*Waris*) can sign the land transfer documents and complete the *Malpot* procedure on your behalf.

## How Remote Land Transfer Works: The Power of Attorney Chain

A land transfer executed from abroad requires a strict legal authentication chain before the Land Revenue Office (*Malpot*) will accept it:


```

[Drafting Warisnama in Nepal] ➔ [Embassy / Consulate Attestation Abroad] ➔ [Physical Courier to Nepal] ➔ [Malpot Office Registration & Transfer]

```

### 1. Special Power of Attorney (*Adhikrit Warisnama*) Drafting
Standard broad or general powers of attorney are often rejected by *Malpot* for property sales. The document must be drafted by a qualified Nepalese legal professional with **explicit details**:
- Exact plot/kitta number (*Kitta No.*), plot area (*Ropani/Aana/Paisa/Daam* or *Bigha/Katha/Dhur*), ward number, and district as recorded in the original **Land Ownership Certificate (*Lalpurja*)**.
- Precise grant of authority allowing the representative to sign the *Rajnama* deed, collect sale funds (or specify bank account transfer details), and receive/hand over the original *Lalpurja*.
- Complete citizenship and passport details of both the principal (landowner) and the designated attorney (*Waris*).

### 2. Execution & Embassy Attestation Abroad
If the landowner is outside Nepal:
- The principal landowner prints the drafted *Warisnama* and visits the nearest **Embassy of Nepal or Consulate General** in their country of residence.
- The landowner signs the document in front of the Consular Officer, who verifies their identity and applies the official diplomatic seal and signature attestation.

### 3. Dispatch & Malpot Registration in Nepal
- The original, wet-ink signed and embassy-stamped document must be physically couriered back to Nepal (digital scans or printouts are **not** accepted by *Malpot*).
- The authorized representative presents the original *Warisnama* at the respective *Malpot Karyalaya* where the land is located to officially register the proxy authority before signing the final land transfer deed (*Rajnama*).

## Document Checklist for Remote Land Transfer

### 📋 From the Overseas Landowner (Principal):
- Duly drafted **Adhikrit Warisnama** (attested by the Nepalese Embassy/Consulate).
- Original or certified true copy of **Nepali Citizenship Certificate (*Nagarikta*)** or NRN Card.
- Copy of valid Passport and current visa/immigration status.
- Recent passport-size photographs.
- Original **Land Ownership Certificate (*Lalpurja*)**.
- Receipt of latest **Land / Property Tax Clearance (*Tiro Tireko Raseed*)** paid to the local municipality/ward office.

### 📋 From the Appointed Representative (*Waris* in Nepal):
- Original Nepali Citizenship Certificate + photocopies.
- Recent passport-size photographs.
- Witness identity documents as required by *Malpot* counters.

## Common Risks & What to Avoid

- **Using Vague Drafts:** If the *Kitta* number, ward number, or specific authority to sell/transfer is omitted or misspelled, *Malpot* will reject the *Warisnama* outright.
- **Attempting Scanned Submissions:** Land Revenue Offices strictly mandate physical, wet-ink original embassy-certified documents.
- **Unpaid Municipality Taxes:** Outstanding local land taxes (*Tiro*) must be cleared up to the current fiscal year before *Naamsari* can be processed.
