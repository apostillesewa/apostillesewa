## Avoiding the Mismatch Trap: Validating Corporate POAs and Board Resolutions for International Markets

When a Nepalese enterprise prepares to set up a subsidiary abroad, enter a joint venture, or open an offshore corporate bank account in financial hubs like Dubai, Singapore, or London, speed and legality are everything. To authorize these international moves, the company must issue two critical legal instruments: a **Board Resolution** and a **Corporate Power of Attorney (POA)**.

While standard agencies treat these like simple paperwork, international corporate registries and foreign embassies do not. In the corporate arena, the most common reason for a sudden embassy rejection is not the lack of a stamp—it is **Individual-to-Corporate Data Mismatch**. 

If the names, signatures, or identification details of your board members do not perfectly sync across the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR), your passport, and the final POA, your international expansion can completely stall.

---

## Why Standard Corporate Legalization Fails

Most businesses assume that getting a stamp from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) ensures global acceptance. However, foreign embassies—particularly those operating out of regional hubs in New Delhi—enforce an entirely different level of scrutiny for commercial legal instruments:

1.  **The Live Registry Cross-Check:** Consular offices do not just verify the MoFA sticker; they verify that the individuals executing the Power of Attorney are legally authorized to do so on behalf of the company according to current **OCR Shareholder and Director Records**.
2.  **The Dual-Identification Rule:** If a Board Resolution is signed by three directors, all three directors' personal credentials (such as passports and citizenship IDs) must be attached, notarized, and aligned perfectly with the company's Article of Association (AoA).
3.  **The Ambiguous Clause Rejection:** International banks and foreign ministries will flatly reject a Corporate POA if the "Scope of Authority" clauses are deemed too broad or ambiguous under the destination country's local corporate laws.

## The Precise Verification Pipeline for Corporate POAs

To ensure a Board Resolution or Power of Attorney holds up under strict international scrutiny, it must pass through an unbroken, legally synchronized verification chain:

[Company Registrar / Chamber of Commerce] ➔ [Line Ministry Verification] ➔ [MoFA Tripureshwor] ➔ [Foreign Embassy / Consulate]
### Step 1: Corporate Execution & Alignment
The Board Resolution must be drafted with precise clauses identifying the exact international actions authorized (e.g., specific bank setups or foreign branch registrations). The signatures of the directors must perfectly match their signatures held on record at the Office of the Company Registrar.

### Step 2: The OCR "Company Profile" Pull
You cannot legalize a POA in isolation. You must attach a freshly pulled, certified **Company Profile (Biwaran)** from the OCR database. This proves to foreign embassies that the board members who signed the resolution are actively registered directors of the company at this exact date.

### Step 3: Specialized Notarization & MoFA
The document moves to a registered notary public who specializes in commercial law, ensuring the physical identity of the signing directors matches their attached passport copies. From there, it is presented to the Consular Services Department at **MoFA in Tripureshwor, Kathmandu**, for central government certification.

### Step 4: Final Consular Legalization
Because many nations handle sovereign processing for Nepal out of India, the complete, audited file—including the POA, Board Resolution, OCR Profile, and Director Passport Copies—is routed directly to the target country's **Embassy or High Commission in New Delhi** for the final diplomatic seal.
