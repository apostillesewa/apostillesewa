When applying for overseas student visas, tourist visas, or education loans in Nepal, embassies and financial institutions require concrete evidence of financial standing and strong home ties. A **Property Valuation Report** serves as official proof of real estate assets owned by the applicant or their financial sponsors.

Prepared on a **desk-based review of official property documents**, this report establishes fair market value based on published government rates (*Nyunatam Mulya*) and regional market transactions.

This guide outlines how desk-based property valuation reports work, required land documents, embassy standards, and how to combine them with notarial certification.

## Required Property Documents Checklist

To issue a valid property valuation report, standard real estate ownership records from the Department of Land Management and Ward Office are reviewed:

### 1. Title & Ownership Proof
* **Lalpurja (Land Ownership Certificate):** Official title deed showing ownership and plot (*kitta*) details.
* **Rajinama (Sale Deed / Transfer Deed):** Historical transfer deed confirming acquisition.
* **Citizenship Certificate:** Government ID of the property owner(s).
* **Charkilla Certificate:** Four-boundary declaration certificate issued by the local Ward Office.
* **Angsabanda (Partition Deed):** Required if the property was acquired via family inheritance partition.

### 2. Survey & Mapping Records
* **Cadastral Map (Blue Print):** Official survey plot blueprint from the Survey Department (*Napi Office*).
* **Trace Map & Field Book Entry:** Identifying plot boundaries and exact area dimensions.

### 3. Building & Construction Permits (If Applicable)
* **Approved Building Plan (Naksha Pass):** Municipal-approved structural blueprint.
* **Building Completion Certificate (*Nirman Sampanna*):** Ward-issued completion clearance.
* **House Numbering Certificate:** Official ward address alignment.

### 4. Tax Clearance Records
* **Land Revenue Receipt (*Tiro Rasid*):** Land revenue tax receipt for the current fiscal year.
* **Property Tax Clearance (*Sampatti Kar*):** Local government property tax clearance certificate.

## 4 Reasons Valuation Reports Get Rejected (And How to Avoid Them)

1. **Unrealistic Over-Valuation:** Unjustified inflated valuations are rejected during embassy scrutiny. Reports must anchor property values to published government base rates (*Nyunatam Mulya*) and verified market comparables.
2. **Unregistered Valuer Signature:** Reports must be signed and sealed by a valuer officially registered under the **Nepal Valuation Act 2019**, complete with active registration credentials.
3. **Expired Validity Dates:** Embassies strictly apply a **6-month freshness rule** for property valuations, while banks prefer reports issued within **3 months**.
4. **Missing Notarial Layer:** Foreign embassies require property valuation reports to carry an official Notary Public seal and attestation on top of the valuer's signature.

3. **Desk Valuation & Drafting:** A registered valuer compiles local rate analysis, land area conversion (Bigha/Kattha/Dhur or Ropani/Aana/Paisa/Daam), and market comparison metrics.
4. **Notarization & Delivery:** The report is signed by the valuer, notarized for foreign embassy acceptance, and delivered digitally (PDF) and via courier (wet-ink hard copies).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Is a physical site visit required for a visa property valuation report?
No. Valuation reports for student and tourist visa applications are desk-based reviews prepared directly from your official property documents (*Lalpurja*, Tax Receipts, and Cadastral Maps).

### Can property located outside Kathmandu be evaluated?
Yes. As long as you provide clear scans of the registered *Lalpurja*, Ward *Charkilla*, and land revenue receipts, desk-based valuation reports can be issued for real estate located anywhere in Nepal.

### How long is a property valuation report valid for visa applications?
Most foreign embassies (including US, UK, Australia, and Canada) accept property valuation reports that are **less than 6 months old** at the time of visa submission.

### Do I need a notarized copy of the valuation report for my embassy interview?
Yes. Most foreign embassies require both the registered valuer's signature/seal and an official Notary Public attestation certifying the report copy.
