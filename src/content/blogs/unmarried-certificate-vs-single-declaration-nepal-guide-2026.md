If you are a Nepali citizen planning to marry abroad, apply for a dependent visa, or settle personal civil registration in a foreign country, you will be required to prove that you are legally free to marry. International authorities and foreign embassies strictly demand proof of single status to prevent bigamy and validate foreign marriage registrations.

However, applicants often get confused between two distinct legal documents: the **Unmarried Certificate (*Vivahit/Avivahit Praman Patra*)** and the **Single Declaration Certificate (Single Status Affidavit)**. Submitting the wrong format can result in immediate rejection by foreign marriage registries or embassy consular sections.

This 2026 guide explains the core differences between these two documents, when each is required, and the step-by-step consular attestation chain needed to legalize them for use overseas.

---

## 1. Core Differences: Unmarried Certificate vs. Single Status Declaration

While both documents serve to prove that you are currently unmarried, they originate from different legal channels and carry different types of legal authority:

+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    DOCUMENT TYPE COMPARISON                            |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| UNMARRIED CERTIFICATE        ➔ Issued by Local Government (Ward/DoCR).  |
|                                 Based on municipal records & recommendation.|
|                                                                         |
| SINGLE STATUS DECLARATION    ➔ Drafted as a Sworn Affidavit under Oath. |
|                                 Witnessed & sealed by a Notary Public.  |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+

| Feature | Unmarried Certificate (*Avivahit Praman Patra*) | Single Declaration Certificate (Affidavit) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Issuing Authority** | Local Ward Office / Rural Municipality | Licensed Notary Public / Legal Counsel |
| **Primary Basis** | Verification of local municipal civil records | Personal declaration made under oath (perjury liability) |
| **Document Format** | Official government certificate format with ward seal | Formatted legal affidavit with sworn jurat clauses |
| **Witnesses Required?** | Yes (Local ward recommendation & family presence) | Yes (Signed in front of the Notary Public) |
| **Primary Destination Use** | Embassies, foreign government civil registries, MOFA attestation | Specific foreign courts, municipal registries (e.g., EU/Nordics), or complex visa files |

> **Key Rule of Thumb:** The Ward-issued **Unmarried Certificate** is the primary legal document recognized by the Nepal Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA). The **Single Status Affidavit** is often required as an additional supporting sworn statement for specific foreign legal jurisdictions.

## 2. When is Each Document Required?

Understanding the requirements of your receiving country or embassy is essential prior to document preparation:
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                          USE CASE BREAKDOWN                             |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| UNMARRIED CERTIFICATE (WARD)       | SINGLE DECLARATION AFFIDAVIT       |
| • Civil Marriage Registrations     | • Legal Marriage in Europe/US/Nordics|
| • Family Reunification Visas       | • Court-Mandated Sworn Statements   |
| • Dependent / Spouse Visa Bundles  | • Supplementary Proof of Single Status|
| • Embassy Consular Files           | • Dual-Citizenship/Immigration Checks|
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+

### Common Scenarios for the Ward-Issued Unmarried Certificate:
* **Foreign Marriage Registrations:** Required by countries like Japan, South Korea, China, UAE, Qatar, and India for registering marriages involving Nepali citizens.
* **Dependent & Spouse Visas:** Included in financial and relationship bundles for UK, USA, Australia, and Schengen spouse applications.
* **Government Consular Authentication:** Serves as the official government document eligible for MOFA attestation in Kathmandu.

### Common Scenarios for a Single Status Affidavit:
* **European & Scandinavian Registries:** Municipalities in countries like Denmark, Germany, France, or Sweden often require a personal sworn declaration under penalty of perjury alongside government certificates.
* **Pre-Marital Legal Clearance:** Required by foreign courts or registrars needing a formal oath confirming no prior undisclosed marriages exist.

## 3. Step-by-Step Legalization Process to the Embassy from Nepal

Because Nepal is not a signatory to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention for most destination countries (such as China, UAE, Qatar, or Russia), your single status document must undergo **Full Diplomatic Consular Legalization**:

+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     FULL LEGALIZATION WORKFLOW                          |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| STEP 1: LOCAL WARD ISSUANCE (Verification & Official Ward Seal)         |
| STEP 2: NOTARY PUBLIC ATTESTATION (For translation or affidavit coupling)|
| STEP 3: NEATS MOFA ATTESTATION (Consular Department, Tripureshwor)       |
| STEP 4: EMBASSY DIPLOMATIC LEGALIZATION (Target Embassy in Kathmandu)    |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+

### Step 1: Local Ward Office Verification & Issuance
1. Visit your local Ward Office (*Woda Karyalaya*) where your permanent address is registered.
2. Submit your original Nepali Citizenship Card, passport copies, passport-sized photos, and witness statements from family members or neighbors.
3. The Ward Officer checks local civil registration records and issues the official **Unmarried Certificate** (in English or bilingual format).

### Step 2: Notarization & Certified English/Foreign Translation
* If the Ward issues the certificate in Nepali, it must undergo **Notarized English Translation** by a recognized professional translator.
* If a Single Declaration Affidavit is required, it is drafted, signed under oath, and certified by a licensed Notary Public.

### Step 3: Nepal MOFA Attestation (Tripureshwor)
All single status certificates must be verified by the **Department of Consular Services** (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) in Kathmandu via the **NEATS (Nepal Electronic Attestation System)** portal:
1. Apply online through the NEATS portal and upload clear scans of your Ward Certificate, Citizenship, and Passport.
2. Attend your scheduled appointment at Tripureshwor for the physical MOFA authentication sticker and signature.

### Step 4: Final Legalization at the Target Embassy
Submit the MOFA-attested document to the destination country's embassy or consular section in Kathmandu (e.g., Chinese Embassy, UAE Embassy, Royal Thai Embassy, etc.):
* The consular officer authenticates the MOFA stamp.
* Once stamped by the embassy, the document is legally recognized for official use in that foreign country.

## 4. Critical Rules & Expiration Deadlines

> **Important Validity Notice:** Single status certificates and affidavits carry strict freshness limits. Most foreign embassies and marriage registrars enforce a strict **3-to-6-month validity window** from the date of issuance. 

### Essential Requirements to Keep in Mind:
1. **Never Apply Too Early:** Do not obtain an Unmarried Certificate months before your scheduled travel or marriage date, as expired documents will be rejected.
2. **Name Consistency:** Ensure your full name, birth date, and parents' names match identically across your Citizenship Card, Ward Certificate, and Passport.
3. **Divorced or Widowed Applicants:** If you were previously married, you cannot use a simple Unmarried Certificate. You must submit your official **Divorce Decree** or **Spouse Death Certificate**, fully verified through MOFA and legalised by the respective embassy.
