## 🚨 CRITICAL NOTICE: Nepal Work Permit (Shram Swikriti) Restrictions for Russia

Before preparing your documents, you must be aware of the Government of Nepal's labor policies regarding travel to Russia:

> **Important Advisory on Employment Visas:** The Department of Foreign Employment (DoFE) and the Ministry of Labour, Employment and Social Security have **suspended the issuance of Foreign Employment Work Permits (*Shram Swikriti*)**—both individual and institutional—for Nepali nationals seeking employment in the Russian Federation. 

### Key Takeaways for Nepali Applicants:
* ❌ **Employment / Work Visas:** Legalizing work experience letters or employment contracts will **not** allow you to obtain a legal *Shram Swikriti* from DoFE to travel to Russia for foreign employment.
* ✅ **Permitted Travel Categories:** Document legalization remains fully accessible and active for **Student Visas (higher education)**, official business visits, non-commercial travel, and specific family or diplomatic procedures.

### Step 1: Issuing Authority Verification
Before any government Ministry handles your document, its origin must be authenticated by the designated governing body:
* **Academic Documents:** Must be verified by the issuing University (e.g., TU, KU, Pokhara University) or the National Examinations Board (NEB) / Centre for Education and Human Resource Development (CEHRD).
* **Civil Status Documents:** Must be issued or certified by the Department of Civil Registration (DoCR) / Local Ward Office.
* **Police Reports:** Must be generated digitally via the Nepal Police clearance system and authenticated by the Police Headquarters.

### Step 2: Nepal MOFA Attestation (Department of Consular Services)
All documents are processed through the **Nepal Electronic Attestation System (NEATS)** managed by the Department of Consular Services in Tripureshwor, Kathmandu.
1. Submit an online application via the official NEATS portal.
2. Upload clear scans of the verified document along with your Nepali Citizenship Card and Passport.
3. Upon electronic approval, attend your scheduled appointment at the Consular Department for physical sticker attestation.

### Step 3: Certified Russian Translation
Russian consular regulations state that foreign-language documents (issued in English or Nepali) submitted to Russian institutions **must be accompanied by an accurate translation into the Russian language**. 

* The translation must faithfully reproduce all text, stamps, seals, and marginal notes.
* The translation must be executed by a certified professional translator to meet Russian notarial compliance standards.

### Step 4: Legalization at the Embassy of the Russian Federation
The final diplomatic stage takes place at the Consular Section of the Embassy of the Russian Federation in Baluwatar, Kathmandu. 
* The consular officer authenticates the signature and seal of the Nepal Ministry of Foreign Affairs official.
* Once stamped, your document is legally valid for submission to Russian universities, government bodies, and legal institutions.

## 5 Critical Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Attempting Embassy Submission First:** The Russian Embassy will immediately reject any Nepali document that does not carry an authentic Nepal MOFA attestation sticker.
2. **Ignoring the Russian Translation Step:** Submitting English-only transcripts or citizenship documents without an official Russian translation will lead to processing delays or administrative rejections in Russia.
3. **Submitting Expired Police Reports:** Police Clearance Certificates (PCC) must be fresh (typically issued within 3 to 6 months of your visa submission).
4. **Name and DOB Discrepancies:** Ensure your full legal name, date of birth, and passport number match identically across your academic records, citizenship card, and passport.
5. **Applying for Work Visas Without Legalizing Clearance:** Fraudulent agents often promise work visas despite DoFE's active ban on work permits for Russia. Verify your visa category before spending money on document processing.
