## Introduction

If you're a Nepali or Bangladeshi national living in Spain and working through Arraigo Social, Arraigo Laboral, long-term residency, or Spanish citizenship, chances are Extranjería has asked for a legalized Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) from your home country. The problem: you can't just walk into an embassy in Spain to get this done, and flying back home — or to New Delhi — could jeopardize your legal stay, your job, or your visa timeline.

Here's the good news: the Embassy of Spain in New Delhi holds regional jurisdiction over legalization for Nepal, Bangladesh, Bhutan, and Sri Lanka. That means your document has a clear, defined path — you just need to follow it correctly, and someone can walk that path on your behalf.

## Key Requirements / What Changed

Both Nepal and Bangladesh route through the Spanish Embassy in New Delhi, but the paths leading there are different:

**Nepal PCC** — a shorter, 4-step chain. Notably, Nepali applicants do **not** need a stamp from the Embassy of Nepal in New Delhi before Spanish legalization — a step other countries in the region do require.

**Bangladesh PCC** — a longer, 5-step chain that includes mandatory stamping by the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi before the Spanish Embassy will process it.

In both cases, the final document must carry a **Traducción Jurada** — a sworn Spanish translation completed by a MAEC-certified translator based in Spain — before it can be submitted to Extranjería.

## Step-by-Step Process

**Nepal:**
1. Online e-PCC issued by Nepal Police Headquarters, Kathmandu
2. Attestation by the Department of Consular Services, MOFA Nepal (Tripureshwor)
3. Legalization at the Embassy of Spain, New Delhi
4. Sworn translation into Spanish, completed in Spain

**Bangladesh:**
1. PCC issued by Bangladesh Police
2. Authentication by MOFA, Dhaka
3. Stamping by the Bangladesh High Commission, New Delhi
4. Legalization at the Embassy of Spain, New Delhi
5. Sworn translation into Spain

A critical detail applicants often miss: PCCs typically carry only a 3–6 month validity window. Any delay in this chain — a missed embassy slot, a rejected authorization letter, a courier delay — can push you past that window, forcing you to restart the entire process from your home country.
