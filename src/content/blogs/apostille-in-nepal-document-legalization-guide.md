## The Confusion Surrounding "Apostille" in Nepal

If you are preparing documents to study, work, or live abroad, you have likely received a clear instruction from a foreign university, employer, or immigration department: **"You need to get your documents Apostilled."**

Your first instinct is to find a government office in Kathmandu that can stamp an Apostille on your birth certificate, marriage certificate, or academic transcripts.

However, there is a major legal catch that surprises thousands of Nepalese citizens every year: **you cannot get a true "Apostille" stamp inside Nepal.** Understanding exactly why — and knowing the correct legal alternative — will save you weeks of delays, wasted money, and potential visa rejections.

---

## The Big Myth: Why Nepal Doesn't Issue Apostilles

An "Apostille" is a specific type of international legalization certificate used between countries that are members of the **1961 Hague Apostille Convention**. When both the originating country and the destination country are members of this convention, a single Apostille sticker makes a document instantly recognized and accepted.

**Here is the reality: Nepal is not a member of the Hague Apostille Convention.**

Because Nepal is not a signatory to this treaty, no government authority inside Nepal has the legal jurisdiction to issue an Apostille stamp. Equally, foreign documents carrying an Apostille sticker are not automatically accepted by Nepalese authorities.

If a foreign country requests an "Apostille" from you, what they actually require is the traditional, multi-tier **Consular Attestation and Embassy Legalization process** — a legally equivalent pathway for non-Hague countries.

---

## The Real Process: Step-by-Step Legalization Chain

Since a shortcut Apostille sticker is not available, your documents must go through a specific chain of government verifications to be deemed legally valid abroad. Here is the standard process:

### Step 1: Notary Public Verification
Your original documents are first verified and certified by a registered Notary Public in Nepal.

### Step 2: Line Ministry / Department Verification
Depending on the document type, it must be verified by its issuing authority:
- **Academic certificates** (SEE/+2/Bachelor's) → Department of Education, National Examinations Board (NEB), or the relevant University.
- **Birth or marriage certificates** → Local Municipal Authority or the Department of Civil Registration.

### Step 3: MoFA Attestation (Tripureshwor, Kathmandu)
Once lower-level departments clear your paperwork, it proceeds to the **Consular Services Department under the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA)** in Kathmandu for official government attestation. This is the highest level of authentication Nepal's government can provide.

### Step 4: Destination Embassy Legalization
This is the final and most critical step. Your MoFA-attested documents must be submitted to the **embassy of your destination country** so they can apply their consular stamp, making the document fully valid for use abroad.

## The New Delhi Embassy Roadblock

While MoFA attestation happens right here in Kathmandu, completing the final step — Embassy Legalization — presents a significant logistical challenge.

Many foreign countries do **not** have embassies or consular offices physically located in Nepal. Instead, they manage diplomatic relations for Nepal through their **regional embassies based in New Delhi, India** — including embassies of many European, Latin American, and African nations.

For an individual, this traditionally means: taking time off work, purchasing flights to India, securing an Indian visa, arranging accommodation in Delhi, and navigating foreign embassy procedures entirely alone — just to obtain a single stamp.
