## Introduction

Applying to a German university through uni-assist, preparing an Ausbildung file, or filing for a visa in Kathmandu all lead to the same question: **what kind of translation does Germany actually accept?**

Online, the answers contradict each other. Some pages say everything must be in German. Others say a Nepal notary stamp is enough. Both claims can cost you weeks. This guide follows what the official sources say: the German Embassy in Kathmandu and uni-assist.

## Myth vs. Fact

| Common claim | What the official sources say |
|---|---|
| "Everything must be translated into German." | The Kathmandu embassy accepts documents in **German or English**, or with a certified translation. uni-assist also accepts **German or English** translations. |
| "Your English transcript needs retranslation." | If your certificates are issued in English or German, uni-assist does not require a translation (check its country notes for exceptions). |
| "A Nepal notary stamp is enough for uni-assist." | uni-assist accepts translations from persons or institutions authorised to translate under oath or admissible in court, or from an authorised department of your school or university. Notary attestation is not named. |
| "A Nepal sworn translator will do." | The embassy states that **there are no sworn translators in Nepal**. It cannot translate documents or notarize third-party translations. |

## Which Authority Is Reading Your Documents?

**1. uni-assist and your university.** You upload certificates in the **original language** plus a translation into German or English. For Nepal, uni-assist asks for your school leaving certificate with subjects and grades (Grade X and XII) and your university diploma with transcript, if you have one. Many universities also ask for officially certified copies of the certificates and of the translations, sent by post.

**2. The German Embassy in Kathmandu.** Its checklists ask for documents in German or English, or with a certified translation, and for Nepali-language documents to be supported by an English or German translation. The embassy also states that German authorities often request verification of documents. Follow the checklist for your exact visa type, whether study, Ausbildung, Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) or skilled worker.

**3. Authorities inside Germany.** Registry offices, immigration offices and courts usually expect translators sworn in Germany.

## Why a Sworn Translator from Germany Is the Safest Route

Because Nepal has no sworn translators recognised by German authorities, the safest choice for uni-assist and official files is a translator who is **sworn in Germany**. Sworn translators are listed in the justice databases of the German states, and the embassy points applicants to those databases. The embassy does not vouch for any translator's quality or cost.

A Nepal notary translation can still help in other situations, for example when a Nepali document must be understood by a Nepali authority or when an authority specifically accepts it. Do not assume it replaces a sworn translation.

## Documents Commonly Translated

* SEE, +2 and Bachelor's certificates, transcripts and character certificates
* Citizenship certificate
* Birth certificate
* Marriage certificate, if applicable
* Police Clearance Certificate, if your file requires it
* Professional or council certificates for skilled-worker routes

The exact list depends on your visa type and university. Always confirm it against the current checklist.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Identify the authority** that will read the documents: uni-assist, the embassy or an authority in Germany.
2. **Check the language of the original.** If your university issued an English transcript, you may not need a translation for uni-assist.
3. **Prepare certified copies** if your university requires them. Never send originals to uni-assist.
4. **Order the translation** from a sworn translator for German or English, depending on the checklist.
5. **Audit names and dates.** Convert Bikram Sambat dates to AD accurately and make sure every name matches your passport.
6. **Assemble the file** in the order the embassy requires, with originals and copies as requested.

## Common Mistakes

* **BS to AD date errors.** A birth date that differs from your passport can cause rejection.
* **Name mismatches.** "Prasad" present in one document and missing in another needs correction before submission.
* **Skipped details.** Seals, registration numbers, handwritten notes and back-page stamps must be translated.
* **Using an ordinary translation agency.** uni-assist does not accept non-certified translations from standard agencies.
* **Paying for unnecessary stamps.** Only complete the legalization steps your checklist actually asks for.
