If you are planning to study, work, conduct business, or complete family reunification in the People's Republic of China, your official Nepali documents must undergo diplomatic attestation. While China acceded to the Hague Apostille Convention in late 2023, **Nepal is not an Apostille signatory country**. Therefore, Nepali documents intended for mainland China still require **Full Diplomatic Consular Legalization**—a multi-tiered authentication chain culminating at the Embassy of the People's Republic of China in Kathmandu.

Whether legalizing academic degrees, relationship certificates, or corporate contracts, following the precise verification sequence avoids costly embassy rejections.

This 2026 guide breaks down the full Chinese document legalization workflow, document-specific application rules, updated fee structures, and critical freshness deadlines.

## Key Document Categories for Legalization

The Consular Section of the Chinese Embassy authenticates both civil and commercial documents issued in Nepal:

### 1. Civil & Family Status Certificates
* **Marriage Certificates:** Must be submitted directly by one of the married parties.
* **Birth Certificates:** Filed with the child listed as the Applicant and the parent/guardian acting as the designated Agent.
* **Death Certificates:** Requires a close relative to apply alongside certified proof of kinship and identity cards for both parties.
* **Relationship Certificates:** Must be accompanied by valid passport or citizenship copies for **every individual** listed on the document.

### 2. Academic & Educational Records
* Bachelor’s / Master’s / PhD Degree Certificates
* Academic Transcripts & Individual Mark Sheets
* Higher Secondary (+2) / SLC / SEE Certificates
* Provisional & Character Certificates

### 3. Background & Commercial Documents
* **Police Clearance Certificates (PCC):** Required for work permits (Z Visa) and residency applications.
* **Corporate Records:** Business Licenses, Articles of Association, Power of Attorney (POA), and Board Resolutions.

## Step-by-Step Chinese Legalization Process in Nepal

+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                   STEP-BY-STEP AUTHENTICATION FLOW                      |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| 1. ISSUER VERIFICATION             | 2. MOFA / NEATS ATTESTATION        |
| • TU, KU, NEB, or CEHRD Stamp      | • Online NEATS portal upload       |
| • Local Ward / DoCR Verification   | • Consular Office (Tripureshwor)   |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| 3. CERTIFIED TRANSLATION           | 4. CHINESE EMBASSY LEGALIZATION    |
| • Chinese or English translation   | • Embassy Consular Section submission|
| • Bound with original prior stamp  | • Final Consular Legalization Stamp|
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+

### Step 1: Issuing Authority Verification
Before government attestation, your document's origin must be validated:
* **Academic Documents:** Certified by the issuing University (e.g., TU, KU) or the National Examinations Board (NEB) / Centre for Education and Human Resource Development (CEHRD).
* **Civil Status Documents:** Certified by the local Ward Office or the Department of Civil Registration (DoCR).
* **Police Reports:** Verified digitally through the Nepal Police clearance system.

### Step 2: Nepal MOFA Attestation (NEATS System)
All authentications proceed through the **Nepal Electronic Attestation System (NEATS)** managed by the Department of Consular Services in Tripureshwor, Kathmandu:
1. File an online application on the NEATS portal.
2. Upload clear scans of the verified document, citizenship card, and passport.
3. Attend your scheduled appointment at the Consular Department for physical sticker attestation.

### Step 3: Embassy-Compliant Translation
The Chinese Embassy requires documents to be understandable by Chinese administrative receiving bodies. Depending on your destination authority in China, foreign-language documents (Nepali/English) may require a certified translation into **Simplified Chinese**.

### Step 4: Chinese Embassy Legalization
The final stage is completed at the Consular Section of the Chinese Embassy in Kathmandu.
* **Visa Center vs. Legalization:** Ordinary visa applications are handled by the Chinese Visa Application Service Center (CVASC), whereas **document legalization is processed directly through the Embassy's Consular Section**.

## Strict Legalization Rules & Document Expiration Standards

To maintain security, the Chinese Embassy enforces strict document integrity checks:

> **Important Validity Warning:** Single-status affidavits (unmarried certificates) and personal certificates intended for **marriage registration in China** are valid for only **six (6) months** from their date of issuance. The embassy will not legalize an expired document.

* **Multi-Page Binding:** Documents containing multiple pages must be properly bound and sealed (e.g., corner flip stamp or ribbon binding). Loose or unbound pages will be rejected immediately.
* **Strict Prohibition of Alterations:** Documents with overwriting, missing seals, or damaged paper are disqualified from legalization.

## 6 Common Errors That Delay Chinese Legalization

1. **Submitting directly to the Chinese Embassy:** The Embassy will reject any document that lacks a physical MOFA attestation sticker from Tripureshwor.
2. **Missing Family Member Passports:** Submitting a Relationship Certificate without providing passport or citizenship copies for **every single family member listed** on the document.
3. **Improper Applicant/Agent Roles:** Submitting a child's birth certificate or a deceased relative's death certificate under incorrect applicant roles without providing legal proof of guardianship or kinship.
4. **Using Expired Single-Status Proof:** Obtaining an unmarried certificate more than 6 months prior to your Chinese embassy appointment.
5. **Confusing Legalization with Visa Processing:** Going to the Chinese Visa Application Center (CVASC) instead of the Embassy Consular Section for document legalization.
6. **Damaged Binding:** Submitting stapled papers where pages have been detached or re-stapled, raising tampering flags.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Does China accept Apostille stamps on Nepali documents?
No. Although mainland China acceded to the Hague Apostille Convention, Nepal is not a signatory country. Therefore, Nepali documents cannot be apostilled for China and must undergo full diplomatic legalization (Local Verification → MOFA Attestation → Chinese Embassy Legalization).

### How long does Chinese Embassy document legalization take in Nepal?
Standard processing through Apostille Sewa—including university checks, NEATS MOFA attestation, translation, and Embassy attestation—typically takes **around 1 week**.

### Who must submit the application for a birth or death certificate?
For a child's birth certificate, a parent or legal guardian acts as the Agent. For a death certificate, a close relative must apply alongside verified proof of kinship (such as a Relationship Certificate) and identification for both parties.

### How long is an unmarried certificate valid for marriage in China?
Single-status affidavits and unmarried certificates intended for marriage registration in China are valid for strictly **six (6) months** from their date of issue.
